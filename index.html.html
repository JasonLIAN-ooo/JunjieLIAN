Research Interests:

Transnational Cinema / Asian Cinema / Creative Industries / Cultural Memory and Heritage / Film Festival

My research focuses on the intersection of transnational cinema and Asian cinema, with particular attention to cultural collisions in co-productions and issues of diaspora and the underclass in film. I am currently writing a paper on In-Laws, Out-Laws (2004), a film that straddles the categories of a Hong Kong–Guangdong film co-production and the first southern New Year film in mainland China. Previously, I conducted research on the innovative development of Guangdong-produced films in the new era as part of a provincial social science project.

I also have a strong interest in cultural memory and heritage. I am concurrently advancing research projects on a Singaporean Kopitiam digital interactive theatre and on the identity of female inheritors of the Qiqiao (The Begging Festival) tradition in Zhucun, Guangdong.

I am also a film industry practitioner, currently serving as a script consultant for the feature film The Debater Comes from a County High School. In addition, I am actively involved in film festivals: I serve as a member of the recommendation committee for the FIRST Fantastic Film Festival and have curated the ORBIT Film Festival.

I expect to graduate with my bachelor's degree in 2027. I am currently applying for MPhil and PhD programs.

Papers

  • Lian, J. (2026). “Looking North” and “Resisting North”: Guangdong–Hong Kong imaginaries in In-Laws, Out-Laws, the first southern New Year film in mainland China. Manuscript under review.

    Supervisor: Hui HU, Associate Professor at South China Agricultural University.

    • Questions: Why did it fare poorly at the Hong Kong box office yet perform relatively well in Guangdong? Why did the “southern” affinity fail to guarantee the success of the Guangdong–Hong Kong film co-production?
    • Method: Drew on a cross-cultural production perspective, reconstructed the entire process through close reading and comparison of the television and film texts combined with related interviews and reports, and analyzed it in the contexts of Lunar New Year film development in Hong Kong and mainland China, Raymond Wong’s personal creative trajectory, and other related contexts.
    • Key Findings: For Hong Kong, not only the “North”, but also the “South” is the Other who is being looked up to and needed to resist. As far as Guangdong is concerned, it has completed a psychological imagination of “identity reversal” in the film, but such an imagination is still not separated from Hong Kong as a reference system, reflecting complex identity anxiety.
    • Innovation: Refined the concept of the mainland to the south of the mainland and the north of the mainland, and provided another analytical path for understanding Hong Kong-ness in the co-productions. And filled the research gap of the southern New Year film in mainland China.

  • Lian, J. (2026). Motivation, practices and space of wander in Zhang Lü’s and Hong Sang-soo’s films. Manuscript under review.

    • Questions: How do the differences in the wanderer’s motivations, practices, and spaces between the Korean-Chinese director and the South Korean director reflect aspects of the experience of modernity in East Asian cinema?
    • Method: Taking Dooman River, Night and Day, and other representative films as texts for comparison, this study comprehensively employs interdisciplinary perspectives from film studies, cultural studies, and other fields to conduct an analysis.
    • Key Findings: Zhang Lü’s films point to a tracing of the past and present a profound sense of history. Hong Sang-soo’s films arise from present desires and manifest an isomorphism between daily space and ethical boundaries. These constitute two forms of the experience of “homelessness” in East Asian cinema: one is “historical homelessness,” and the other is “everyday homelessness.”
    • Innovation: This study enriched the comparative map of experiences of cinematic modernity in East Asia.

Research on the Innovative Development of Guangdong-produced Film and Television Dramas in the New Era

Jul. 2025 – Present

Regular Project of the Guangdong Provincial Philosophy and Social Sciences Planning Programme
Supervisor: Hui HU, Associate Professor at South China Agricultural University.

  • Synopsis
    This project investigates representative cases of Guangdong-produced film and television dramas from 2012 to 2024 and analyzes their innovative development strategies to provide practical references for their future high-quality development.
  • Main Contributions
    • Analyzed the official promotional accounts of 12 works and found that, beyond thematic expression, some works similarly suffered from the predicament of “placelessness” in platform dissemination, mainly manifested in fragmentation and self-ossification. It addressed the issue of strong consumer demand and weak production capacity from a communication perspective.
    • Reviewed the current state of research and policy documents, identified existing research gaps, and led the drafting of the literature review, thereby delineating the project’s entry point and innovative direction.

Cultural Heritage & Other Tracks

A Digital Interactive Theatre of Cultural Artefacts Set in the Singaporean Kopitiam

Sep. 2026 – Present

Interdisciplinary Self-Organizing Cultural Relics Research Project.

  • Synopsis
    This project uses the digital interactive theatre to make cultural relics perceptible rather than constructed, to show their autonomy. The scene is set in the Singaporean Kopitiam, a coffee shop, to discuss the relationships between migration, labor, and public space. It is currently in the narrative-building stage.
  • Main Contributions
    • Reviewed the literature and organized existing museum collection records and other materials, placed coffee shops back into colonial trade and other structures for examination, avoided obfuscation by nostalgic narratives, and narrowed the topic no longer too broad.
    • Started from questions such as “which objects might appear in the same scene,” refined the selection criteria, proposed a method of linking stories through core objects, solved the problem of fragmented stories caused by too many objects, and advanced the project to the verification stage of “which objects are related to one another.”

Preserving the Lineage in Images: Visual Fieldwork on the Qiqiao Intangible Cultural Heritage in Zhucun, Guangdong

Jul. 2026 – Present

Postgraduate Education Innovation Project, South China Agricultural University
Supervisor: Difei TANG, Associate Professor at South China Agricultural University.

  • Synopsis
    This project examines how female inheritors of the Qiqiao customs of the Pan lineage acquire legitimacy within the lineage system and conversely reshape it.   • Main Contributions

    • Conducted four preliminary field visits, interviewed 12 individuals informally, pre-interviewed seven individuals, and led the development of a strategy focused on “building rapport with key internal informants and preliminary participatory observation,” successfully inviting four female inheritors to participate in the co-creation of video archives.

Social Service

As a debater with nine years of experience, and a graduate of a county high school in Zhaoqing, Guangdong Province, China, I have been dedicated to developing youth debate education in remote areas since 2023, striving to make debate less elitist. I believe critical thinking should not be the cultural capital of a specific class but an intellectual resource accessible to every young person. Through the Guangdong Province Three Goes to the Countryside social practice program, I connected university debate teams in Guangdong with three middle schools in Guangning County, providing debate training to their students. Additionally, I served as a debate coach for four high schools in Zhaoqing, guiding them to more than 20 achievements, including runner up in the Guangdong Provincial Youth Chinese Debate Championship, gold award in the Chinese Debate World Cup, and third place in the Nanhai Cup International Chinese Debate Invitational Tournament for Primary and Secondary School Students.

In practice, I found that the high registration fees for current middle school debate competitions essentially constitute a hidden entry barrier. Students from economically underdeveloped regions have long been excluded from high quality competitions. Even when they access excellent learning resources through the internet, their visibility and pathways for advancement remain extremely limited. Therefore, in 2025 I founded the Nanyun Cup International Middle School Chinese Debate Championship and established a separate division for economically underdeveloped regions. Over two editions, it has attracted twenty teams, including teams from Guangxi, Guizhou, and other underdeveloped provinces in China.

Furthermore, as a film studies researcher, I fully recognize that film, as a cultural practice, possesses a public participatory quality. It can respond to public social issues and help drive social progress. I facilitated the film and television rights cooperation for the renowned Southern Weekly feature article The Best Debater Comes from an Unknown County High School. This feature article documented the journey of a group of county high school students from Guangning County who overcame numerous obstacles to reach an international debate stage. The film is scheduled to begin shooting in 2027, directed by Xu Yizhou, the screenwriter of Take Off (2026), a film that was selected for the main competition of the 38th Tokyo International Film Festival. In this project, I serve as a script consultant. In addition to providing technical guidance on the debate sections, I have contributed suggestions from the perspectives of film narrative and literary expression, translating my professional training into concrete creative practice. This is consistent with the goal I have long pursued: to let the voices of marginalized groups be heard and to bring subjects excluded from dialogue back into the conversation.
